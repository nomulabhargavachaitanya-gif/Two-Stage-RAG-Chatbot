Octopuses are marine animals known for their intelligence, flexible bodies, and eight arms. They live in oceans around the world, usually sheltering in rocky crevices, coral reefs, or other protected spaces. Most species are solitary and spend much of their time searching for crabs, fish, and other small prey.

An octopus can change the color and texture of its skin to blend into its surroundings or communicate with another octopus. This ability is controlled by specialized cells called chromatophores, along with other structures in the skin that reflect and scatter light. Camouflage helps the animal avoid predators and approach prey.

Octopuses are also skilled problem solvers. They can open containers, navigate mazes, and learn from experience. Because they have no internal skeleton, they can squeeze through surprisingly small openings, provided the opening is large enough for their beak, which is the hardest part of their body.
