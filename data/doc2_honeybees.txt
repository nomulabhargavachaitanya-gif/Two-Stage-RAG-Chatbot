Honeybees are eusocial insects belonging to the genus Apis that live in highly structured colonies comprising three distinct castes: a single reproductive queen, tens of thousands of sterile female workers, and several hundred male drones. The queen bee is the mother of nearly all individuals in the hive and secretes Queen Mandibular Pheromone (QMP) to maintain colony cohesion and suppress the development of worker ovaries. Drone bees develop from unfertilized eggs through arrhenotokous parthenogenesis and exist solely to mate with virgin queens from other colonies during nuptial flights, dying immediately upon successful copulation.

Female worker bees perform tasks that change progressively as they age, a behavioral organization known as temporal polyethism. Newly emerged workers clean cells and nurse developing brood, feeding larvae royal jelly produced by their hypopharyngeal glands. Middle-aged workers construct hexagonal wax combs using eight pairs of abdominal wax glands, process nectar into honey, and guard the hive entrance against intruders. Older workers serve as field foragers, navigating several kilometers to gather floral nectar, protein-rich pollen, propolis tree resin, and fresh water.

Honeybee communication relies extensively on ritualized dances deciphered by ethologist Karl von Frisch. When a profitable floral patch is located within 50 meters, returning foragers perform a circular round dance. For distances exceeding 50 meters, foragers execute the figure-eight waggle dance on vertical comb faces. The angle of the central waggle run relative to vertical gravity encodes the direction of the food source relative to the sun's current azimuth. The duration of the waggle phase communicates distance, with roughly one second of waggle vibration corresponding to approximately one kilometer of flight distance. Additionally, workers maintain hive brood temperatures precisely between 32°C and 35°C by shivering their flight muscles to generate heat or fanning wings and evaporating water droplets during excessive warmth.
